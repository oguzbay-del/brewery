## 1. Strategic message house

### 1.1 Core positioning

**Demleme is brewing software designed around the real work of making and producing beer — with technical methods, clear records and distinct workflows for homebrewers and professional breweries.**

This is a positioning statement, not a claim that every described workflow is currently available. Every capability must retain its product-plan stage.

### 1.2 Message hierarchy

| Level | Message | Supporting explanation | Proof required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core promise | Bring more clarity and consistency to brewing work | Organize brewing decisions, measurements and production records in workflows appropriate to the user | Product scope and approved workflow design |
| Pillar 1 — Technical clarity | Understand the numbers behind brewing decisions | Explain methods, formulas, units, assumptions and limitations | Formula references, independent test vectors, validation record |
| Pillar 2 — Connected workflow | Connect recipes, batches, measurements and records | Show the relationship from recipe planning through execution and review | Approved data model and workflow specification |
| Pillar 3 — Fit for two audiences | Homebrewing and professional production need different workflows | Separate Home and Business pages, examples, roles and terminology | Product-plan audience mapping |
| Pillar 4 — Operational discipline | Make plans and actual records easier to compare | Explain records, versions, quality and traceability where planned | Product-plan scope, stage labels and implemented evidence |
| Trust foundation | Be explicit about what exists, what is planned and what is not included | No invented launch dates, results, customers, integrations or guarantees | Claim register and release verification |

### 1.3 What Demleme should not claim

- “The most accurate”, “industry-leading”, “revolutionary” or “best” without comparative evidence.
- “All-in-one brewery management” or ERP positioning.
- Automated prediction, AI optimization or recommendations unless approved and validated.
- Guaranteed consistency, yield, savings, profitability, compliance or quality outcomes.
- Live integration, automation, security certification, uptime or regulatory compliance without implementation evidence.
- Available, free, trial, signup or purchase language for product flows that do not exist.

## 2. Audience-specific value propositions

### 2.1 Homebrewers

**Value proposition direction:** A clearer way to plan recipes, understand brewing calculations, record brew days and learn from each batch.

| Message component | Direction |
|---|---|
| Audience | Homebrewers who care about understanding process variables and repeatability |
| Main job | Formulate, calculate, brew, measure and review |
| Problem | Brewing information and calculations are scattered; it is difficult to connect recipe intent with measured results |
| Intended value | A connected, understandable workflow for recipe and batch information |
| Differentiator | Technical explanations and transparent calculation methods, not a black-box number |
| Emotional register | Confidence through understanding; curiosity and control |
| Evidence | Formula sources, reference examples, units, assumptions, batch workflow design |
| Avoid | “Perfect beer every time”, guaranteed improvement, advanced automation as a default |

**Homebrewer message pillars**
1. Plan a recipe with its ingredients, targets and brewing context.
2. Understand the calculations and assumptions behind key brewing decisions.
3. Keep brew-day plans and measured results connected.
4. Record fermentation and batch observations within the supported product scope.
5. Learn from previous batches without overstating automated insights.

**Home-specific headline territory (directional, not approved final copy)**
- “Plan your brew. Understand every number.”
- “A clearer way to design, brew and learn.”
- “Bring your recipes, calculations and batch records together.”

Preferred direction: technical clarity + complete brewing journey. Avoid generic “brew better beer” claims unless supported by measurable evidence.

### 2.2 Professional Breweries

**Value proposition direction:** A structured way to connect recipes, production planning, batch records and quality workflows for professional brewing teams.

| Message component | Direction |
|---|---|
| Audience | Small/professional breweries and their owners, head brewers, operators and quality staff |
| Main job | Standardize, approve, schedule, produce, inspect and trace |
| Problem | Recipe intent, production execution, tank/lot information and quality records can become fragmented |
| Intended value | Better-connected production workflows and records within the defined brewing scope |
| Differentiator | Brewing-specific workflow depth and explicit boundaries rather than generic ERP claims |
| Emotional register | Control, accountability, repeatability and operational clarity |
| Evidence | Workflow specification, data relationships, role/permission design, record history |
| Avoid | “Run your entire brewery”, ERP, accounting, sales, distribution or compliance guarantees |

**Business message pillars**
1. Maintain recipe versions and review/approval workflows.
2. Connect production planning with batches and tank allocation at the relevant roadmap stage.
3. Keep production plans and actual records distinguishable.
4. Relate ingredient lots, batches and quality records where planned.
5. Make scope boundaries and workflow maturity explicit.

**Business-specific headline territory (directional, not approved final copy)**
- “Bring structure to your brewing operations.”
- “Connect recipes, production and batch records.”
- “Brewing-specific workflows for professional teams.”

Preferred direction: recipe-to-production continuity and traceability. Do not use “complete brewery ERP” or imply financial, commercial and distribution workflows.

## 4. Page-by-page content briefs

### 4.1 Home page — `/`

**Intent:** Brand/category discovery and audience routing.  
**Primary commercial audience:** Professional Breweries (Business). **Secondary audience:** Homebrewers (Home).  
**Primary message:** Brewing-specific software with clear workflows for homebrewers and professional breweries.  
**Required sections:** Hero, audience split, lifecycle overview, feature groups, technical principles, Learn/Calculators, product status, FAQ preview.  
**Proof:** Product-plan alignment; no customer proof required.  
**SEO focus:** Demleme brewing software; brewing software for homebrewers and breweries (natural language only).  
**CTA:** Primary — Explore professional breweries. Secondary — Explore homebrewing.  
**Do not:** Make pricing, launch, customer or availability claims.

### 4.2 Homebrewers — `/homebrewers/`

**Intent:** Homebrewing software evaluation.  
**Primary message:** Plan recipes, understand calculations and connect brew-day and batch records.  
**Required sections:** Who it is for, brewing lifecycle, recipe/calculation topics, water/mash/yeast, brew day, fermentation, technical trust, platform/device boundaries, FAQ.  
**Proof:** Validated formulas and workflow design.  
**SEO focus:** Homebrewing software; homebrew recipe management; brewing calculations.  
**CTA:** Explore recipe workflow / Read a brewing guide / Use a verified calculator.  
**Do not:** Imply professional tank/quality workflows are Home defaults or claim predictions that remain unapproved.

### 4.3 Breweries — `/breweries/`

**Intent:** Professional brewery software evaluation.  
**Primary message:** Connect recipe management, production planning and brewing records for professional teams.  
**Required sections:** Intended brewery/team, production lifecycle, roles, production planning, records, inventory/lot traceability, QC, explicit exclusions, platform/security status, FAQ.  
**Proof:** Product scope, data relationships and approved operational workflows.  
**SEO focus:** Brewery production software; brewery recipe management; brewery batch tracking.  
**CTA:** Explore production workflow / Review product scope.  
**Do not:** Claim ERP, accounting, sales, shipping, keg management, automated regulatory filing or certification.

### 4.4 Features — `/features/` and detail pages

**Intent:** Capability evaluation.  
**Message:** Each capability is explained in the context of a user problem and a real brewing workflow.  
**Required fields:** Audience, problem, intended capability, workflow, inputs, outputs/records, status, dependencies, limitations, related resources.  
**Proof:** Every statement mapped to the product plan and WP-03 status.  
**CTA:** Related audience workflow + relevant technical guide.  
**Do not:** Present stage labels as release promises or mark a capability Available without verification.

### 4.5 How It Works — `/how-it-works/`

**Intent:** Understand end-to-end product logic.  
**Message:** The same brewing lifecycle has different needs in Home and Business.  
**Required sections:** Separate Home and Business journey diagrams, stage purpose, inputs, records, product status and related feature links.  
**Proof:** Product plan, no fabricated UI.  
**CTA:** Explore the relevant audience page.  
**Do not:** Depict planned screens as live product screenshots.

### 4.6 Calculators — `/calculators/` and detail pages

**Intent:** Complete a brewing calculation and understand its method.  
**Message:** Useful calculations should be transparent, testable and explicit about their assumptions.  
**Required sections:** Task definition, input units, output units, formula/method, worked example, boundary behavior, limitations, references, related guide.  
**Proof:** Independent test vectors, golden examples, reference sources, calculation-engine consistency decision.  
**CTA:** Calculate / How this is calculated / Read the related guide.  
**Do not:** Publish unvalidated formulas, suggest web tools are connected to the product account, or hide uncertainty.

### 4.7 Learn — `/learn/` and articles

**Intent:** Answer a technical brewing question.  
**Message:** Explain brewing concepts accurately and connect them to practical decisions.  
**Required sections:** Direct answer, explanation, method, units, examples, caveats, sources, related topics and product connection.  
**Proof:** Primary/authoritative references and technical review.  
**CTA:** Related guide, validated calculator or feature page.  
**Do not:** Use thin SEO copy, unsupported advice or invented expert authorship.

### 4.8 Use cases — `/use-cases/` and details

**Intent:** See whether Demleme's planned workflows map to a real problem.  
**Message:** Show a concrete brewing scenario and the information/decisions involved.  
**Required sections:** User, context, problem, inputs, steps, expected records, feature status, limitations and related resources.  
**Proof:** Product-plan mapping; quantified outcome only with real evidence.  
**CTA:** Relevant audience workflow and feature.  
**Do not:** Invent ROI, savings, quality improvement or customer stories.

### 4.9 FAQ — `/faq/`

**Intent:** Resolve objections and product-scope questions.  
**Message:** Direct, honest answers about capability, status, platform, data, calculations and exclusions.  
**Proof:** Product plan, privacy/legal review and release verification.  
**CTA:** Link to exact supporting page.  
**Do not:** Guess launch dates, prices, plan limits or security posture.

### 4.10 Technical Principles — `/technical-principles/`

**Intent:** Evaluate technical trust.  
**Message:** Explain how methods, units, validation, records and data principles are approached.  
**Required sections:** Formula transparency, unit systems, rounding, validation approach, data model principles, security statements with evidence, known limitations.  
**Proof:** Test methodology, technical references and implementation evidence.  
**CTA:** Read formula explainer / Explore relevant feature.  
**Do not:** Equate design intent with deployed controls or certification.

### 4.11 About / Roadmap / Pricing

- About: product philosophy, audience and scope. Avoid invented company history or team credentials.
- Roadmap: publish only if product owner approves public status vocabulary and disclosure. No dated commitments by default.
- Pricing: hold until actual commercial model, limits, billing and terms are approved. Do not create placeholder price tables.

## 5. Proof and evidence matrix

| Claim category | Acceptable evidence | Evidence not sufficient | Publishing rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calculation accuracy | Formula source, independent reference values, golden tests, boundary tests | “Industry standard” phrase or internal assertion | No accuracy claim without documented validation |
| Recipe/style support | Exact style guide/version and supported field mapping | Generic “supports all styles” | Name exact guides and version |
| Device integration | Tested model/protocol, direction, firmware constraints, supported stage | Product idea, mock-up or device logo | Name exact supported integration only |
| Production/tank workflow | Approved scope and tested workflow | Roadmap mock-up alone | Use Planned/In development until released |
| Traceability | Defined lot/batch data relationships and working record behavior | Conceptual diagram alone | Describe design intent until implemented |
| Security/privacy | Implemented controls, policies, review or certification evidence | Architecture plan alone | State principles, not deployed guarantees |
| Regulatory/compliance | Current official jurisdiction sources and specialist review | General knowledge or another country's rule | No compliance guarantee; jurisdiction-specific caveats |
| Customer outcomes | Verifiable customer approval, methodology and context | Hypothetical case or anonymous invented quote | No testimonial or quantified result without proof |
| Product availability | Deployed route, tested account/access flow and release sign-off | Feature roadmap or code branch | “Available” only after release verification |
| Pricing/trial | Approved commercial decision and working purchase flow | Competitor price or placeholder | No public pricing until approved |

## 6. Terminology and editorial style guide

### 6.1 Brand and product naming

- Product name: **Demleme**.
- Use “Demleme” consistently; do not invent alternate brand spellings or product tiers.
- Audience labels: “Homebrewers” and “Professional Breweries”.
- Internal shorthand “Home” and “Business” may appear in planning documents; public pages should use audience labels or explain the mode clearly.
- “Brewery management” must be qualified as brewing production workflows, not full business ERP.

### 6.2 Preferred terminology

| Concept | Preferred public term | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Home user | Homebrewer | Avoid “amateur” |
| Professional customer | Professional brewery / brewing team | Do not invent size threshold |
| Recipe | Recipe | Use consistently |
| Batch | Batch | Define where Home and Business data differ |
| Brew day | Brew day (US); brewday may appear in UK technical sources | Choose one house style per page |
| Original gravity | Original gravity (OG) | Define at first use |
| Final gravity | Final gravity (FG) | Define at first use |
| Alcohol by volume | Alcohol by volume (ABV) | Explain estimate vs measured value |
| International Bitterness Units | International Bitterness Units (IBU) | Explain model assumptions |
| Colour | Color (US) / colour (UK) | US default site; use natural UK variants in UK content |
| Aluminum | Aluminum (US) / aluminium (UK) | Technical content can note variant |
| Fermenter / tank | Fermenter for home context; tank / vessel for professional context | Avoid flattening audience distinction |
| Lot | Ingredient lot | Keep separate from batch |
| Traceability | Lot-to-batch traceability | Only where planned/implemented |
| Planned vs actual | Planned and actual values/records | Avoid implying automatic measurement |
| Calibration | Calibration status / calibration record | Claim stage conflict in WP-03 must be resolved |

### 6.3 Units and localization

- English-first site with US as default editorial spelling.
- Technical calculations should support and clearly label relevant unit systems; never silently mix US customary, Imperial and metric.
- Distinguish US gallons from Imperial gallons.
- Show units next to every input and output; include conversions where validated.
- Keep °C/°F, L/gal, kg/lb, g/oz and gravity formats explicit.
- Explain specific gravity, Plato and other scales where relevant.
- Number formatting and decimal separators must be locale-aware in functional calculators.
- Do not translate technical units or formulas inconsistently between article and calculator.

### 6.4 Tone and style

- Precise, calm, practical and technically literate.
- Prefer concrete verbs: formulate, calculate, record, compare, review, schedule, trace.
- Explain jargon when first introduced; do not oversimplify expert concepts.
- Avoid hype, exaggerated adjectives, fake urgency and vague claims.
- Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings, tables for comparisons and step sequences for workflows.
- State uncertainty and limitations plainly.
- Never write as if a planned capability is already deployed.

## 8. Content production workflow

Every page follows this workflow:

1. **Brief:** audience, search intent, page role, target question and internal links.
2. **Claim map:** every capability statement linked to product plan and WP-03 status.
3. **Evidence pack:** sources, formulas, product decisions, screenshots (only if real and approved).
4. **Outline:** section purpose and proof needed.
5. **Draft:** direct, technically clear and audience-specific.
6. **Technical review:** formula, terminology, units, device, security or regulatory check as applicable.
7. **SEO review:** unique intent, title, description, H1, canonical and links.
8. **Product-owner review:** status, scope and CTA accuracy.
9. **Publish gate:** only after evidence and destination are ready.
10. **Maintenance:** review technical articles and jurisdiction-sensitive content on a defined schedule.

### Content record fields

Each content item should have:
- Stable content ID and route.
- Audience (Home / Business / Both).
- Search intent and primary topic.
- Product-plan references.
- Claim status and evidence links.
- Technical reviewer / owner.
- Last reviewed date.
- Related feature, calculator, FAQ and use case.
- Publication state (brief / draft / review / approved / published).
