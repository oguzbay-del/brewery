# GRISTORA — Trademark counsel review brief

**Prepared:** 15 November 2026  
**Status:** Internal briefing for qualified trademark counsel; not a legal opinion or filing instruction.  
**Decision sought:** Whether GRISTORA is a viable public brand for brewing software in the intended territories, subject to appropriate goods/services wording and domain clearance.

## 1. Proposed mark and business

- Word mark: GRISTORA
- Descriptor: Brewing Intelligence
- Product category: brewing software / hosted software service for professional breweries; homebrewers are a secondary audience.
- Commercial approach: self-service software access.
- Initial target markets: United States and United Kingdom; Australia and WIPO/Madrid screening. EU and Türkiye are additional relevant-market checks.
- Likely goods/services: hosted SaaS for brewery production/recipe/batch/measurement workflows; downloadable software only if branded and actually distributed.
- No planned general-purpose ERP, accounting, CRM, shipping, sales-order or distribution services.

## 2. Highest-priority name lead: C-DAC GistOra Tools (India)

C-DAC's official website currently lists **GistOra Tools**, described as a server-side database application development tool for Indian-language applications. The page says it works within Oracle and supports desktop/LAN and web applications:
- https://www.cdac.gov.in/index.aspx?id=mc_ilf_gistora
- https://www.cdac.gov.in/index.aspx?id=mlc_gist_products

Why this matters:
- “GistOra” differs from “GRISTORA” by the initial letter R.
- The names are visually and phonetically close.
- Both are software-related; C-DAC's listed product is itself an application-development tool.
- The C-DAC page is live on an official institutional website.

What is not yet known:
- First-use date and whether the product is still commercially active.
- Whether C-DAC owns, licenses or has registered GistOra as a trademark.
- Actual markets, sales channels, users, goodwill and current scope of use.
- Whether any rights or international registrations exist outside India.

Counsel should prioritize a review of prior/current use, trademark records and likelihood of confusion, including whether the distinction created by the initial R is sufficient for the planned software/SaaS services.

## 4. Preliminary goods/services scope

Document 40 contains a proposed wording draft. Current working assumptions:
- **Class 42:** likely core for hosted SaaS / online non-downloadable brewery software.
- **Class 9:** include only if GRISTORA-branded downloadable software or a mobile app is actually distributed.
- **Class 35:** not automatic merely because the software helps a brewery manage its own business; include only if the provider actually offers covered business-management/administration services.
- **Class 41:** only if separately marketed training/education services are planned.

Counsel should refine the wording for each territory, identify relevant adjacent classes and avoid overbroad claims.

## 5. Search work required

1. Search GRISTORA, GRISTOR, GISTORA, GistOra, GRISTORA-like spelling and pronunciation variants in USPTO, UK IPO, IP Australia and WIPO/Madrid.
2. Search related marks in relevant classes and related goods/services; assess phonetic and visual similarity.
3. Search C-DAC GistOra prior use and rights, including India register and any international filings or territorial use.
4. Verify BASF GISTORA application DID2026030217 in DGIP after the publication window and application 7525714 in official IP India.
5. Check common-law commercial use in brewing, software, analytics, process control and adjacent technology sectors.
6. Capture current RDAP/registrar evidence for gristora.com, gristora.ai and gristora.io; current status remains unknown.
7. Provide written assessment by territory: registrability, infringement/likelihood-of-confusion risk, prior-use exposure, filing strategy and any recommended alternative/limitation.

## 6. Questions for counsel

- Is GRISTORA sufficiently distinguishable from C-DAC's GistOra Tools for hosted brewing software, particularly in India and internationally?
- Does C-DAC's listed use establish relevant prior rights, and what evidence is needed to establish current use, goodwill or enforceability?
- Do the BASF GISTORA filings in Classes 1/5/31 create any meaningful risk for software in Classes 9/42, considering phonetic similarity and the actual goods/services?
- Which classes and exact goods/services wording should be filed in the US, UK and Australia?
- Should the owner seek a word mark first, a device/logo mark later, or both, and in what order?
- Are there any limitations, coexistence, consent or naming changes counsel would recommend before public launch?

## 7. Current decision posture

**Conditional hold — no legal clearance conclusion.** C-DAC GistOra Tools is a material software-sector prior-use lead requiring counsel review. BASF's GISTORA filings are additional watch leads in agricultural classes. Official US/UK/Australia/WIPO record-level results and live domain RDAP evidence have not yet been captured.

No filing, domain purchase, third-party contact or public statement of clearance is authorised by this brief.
